An anti-fraud back-office built around the analyst's loop

Rules decide on every payment. What they can't decide goes to people, and what people find goes back into the rules through the rule builder. I designed the back-office of ScanFraud so that this is one continuous path: from the payment stream, to the transaction, to everything connected to it, to the blacklist.

01 · Context

Rules handle the volume. People handle the rest, and teach the rules

  • Approve · decline · review
  • Analyst loop
  • Back into the rules

ScanFraud scores every payment of a payment provider's merchants and returns a reaction: approve, decline, send for review. Most payments never need a person. The ones that do land in the back-office, where a risk analyst has to answer two questions quickly: is this fraud, and what else is connected to it?

The second answer matters more. A blocked card stops one payment; a blocked phone, device or customer stops the whole ring behind it. So the back-office isn't a set of separate admin pages. It is one path: Monitoring → transaction → Cross-check → blacklist.

02 · Monitoring

Every scored payment, narrowed to the ones that need a person

  • All · For review · Viewed
  • Filters as chips

Monitoring opens on the live stream with three views: All, For review and Viewed. Filters stay visible as removable chips above the table, so an analyst always sees what the list is narrowed to: status, fraud flag, method, merchant, amount, score, list. Rows can be selected for bulk actions, columns can be configured, and any slice exports to CSV.

03 · Transaction

The decision first, the evidence after

  • Drawer over the list
  • Reaction first
  • Section index

The eye in a Monitoring row opens the transaction in a drawer over the list, so an analyst can go through payments one by one without losing their place. Details in the drawer opens the full page.

Both answer "why" before "what". The full page opens on Reaction & Triggered Entities: the reaction, its description, and which rule fired on which merchant, service, terminal or provider. Then come the overview, payment method, customer, device, entities, sanctions matches, the rules that ran, and the history of what analysts did with it. A section index on the left gets to any of them in one click.

Values that are already on a blacklist are marked in place, so the analyst sees the connection without leaving the card. The actions that move the case on sit in the header: Reopen, Viewed, Crosscheck, Blacklist, Fraud status.

04 · Cross-check

From one payment to everything that shares its details

  • 8 parameters
  • All or any
  • Exclude a parameter

Cross-check takes the parameters of the payment (card, customer, email, phone, device, IP, fingerprint, cardholder) and shows how many other transactions share each one. The analyst matches on all of them or any of them, can exclude a parameter from the search, and sees approved and declined totals at the top. Selected transactions get a fraud status or go onto a blacklist in bulk, and a partial-success dialog lists exactly which values were added and which weren't.

05 · Dictionaries

Reference lists, and imports that don't drop values silently

  • Reference lists
  • Analyze step
  • Needs attention

Dictionaries are plain reference lists, such as sets of countries. Countries can be added by hand or by import, and imports go through an Analyze step: values the system recognises are matched, and the ones it doesn't are collected under "Needs attention" instead of being dropped without a word.
